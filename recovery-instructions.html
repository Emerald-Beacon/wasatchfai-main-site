Post-Operative Care

Recovery Instructions

General guidance for the first few days after a foot or ankle procedure. Your care team will give you instructions specific to your surgery — use this as a general reference.

Elevation & swelling management

Elevate your foot or ankle above the level of your heart as much as possible during the first few days. Placing pillows underneath your leg while resting or sleeping helps reduce swelling and pain.

Bandage & dressing care

Do not remove your bandage unless your podiatrist instructs you to — you may loosen the outer layer if it feels too tight, but keep the dressing underneath in place. Keep your bandage dry; wrapping your foot or ankle in a plastic bag or using a cast protector helps during bathing.

Pain control

Apply ice in roughly 10-minute intervals for the first day or two after your procedure to help manage swelling and discomfort. Take your prescribed pain medication as directed, especially during the first two to three days when pain is typically most noticeable.

Activity restrictions

Do not drive or operate hazardous machinery for at least 24 hours after your procedure or while taking narcotic pain medication. Arrange for a responsible adult to accompany you for the first 24 hours after anesthesia, and avoid strenuous exercise until your podiatrist clears you to resume normal activity.

Diet after your procedure

Start with light, easy-to-digest foods on the day of your procedure and progress to your normal diet the following day. It's best to avoid hot, spicy, or greasy foods for the first two to three days as your body adjusts.

When to call us

Call our office with any questions about your recovery. Seek emergency care right away if you notice signs of infection, uncontrolled bleeding, severe pain that isn't relieved by medication, or numbness that doesn't improve. For recovery guidance specific to your procedure, visit our general recovery page.

Common Questions

Recovery instructions FAQs

How long should I keep my foot elevated?

Most patients are asked to elevate their foot or ankle above heart level as much as possible for the first several days after surgery. Your podiatrist will let you know when you can reduce elevation based on your specific procedure.

Can I get my bandage wet?

Keep your bandage dry unless told otherwise. Use a plastic bag or cast protector over the dressing when bathing, and avoid soaking the area until your podiatrist says it's safe.

How do I manage pain after my procedure?

Ice in short intervals during the first day or two, along with taking your prescribed pain medication as directed, typically provides the most relief during the first two to three days.

When can I drive again?

Avoid driving for at least 24 hours after your procedure or while taking narcotic pain medication. Your podiatrist will tell you when it's safe to resume driving based on your specific surgery.

What symptoms mean I should call the office?

Call us if you notice signs of infection, uncontrolled bleeding, pain that isn't relieved by medication, or numbness that doesn't improve. These can be signs that need prompt evaluation.
