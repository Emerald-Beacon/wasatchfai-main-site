Hallux Rigidus

A big toe that's growing stiffer and more painful with every step — we diagnose the stage of arthritis and build a plan to keep you moving.

What is hallux rigidus?

Hallux rigidus is a condition that causes pain and stiffness in the joint at the base of the big toe. It is a form of degenerative arthritis that develops as the toe's range of motion gradually decreases over time. Because it involves ongoing wear at the joint, it tends to progress in stages, and early recognition allows for more treatment options before the joint becomes severely restricted.

Hallux rigidus is sometimes confused with a bunion because both conditions affect the same joint at the base of the big toe. However, they are different problems — a bunion is a bony bump caused by a shift in joint alignment, while hallux rigidus is a stiffening, arthritic change within the joint itself. If you are not sure which condition matches your symptoms, our bunion removal page can help you compare.

What are the stages of hallux rigidus?

Hallux rigidus is a progressive condition. In its earlier stage, sometimes called "hallux limitus," the big toe has limited motion but is not yet completely restricted. As the condition advances toward true hallux rigidus, the joint becomes increasingly stiff, eventually resulting in what is often described as a "frozen joint" with very little remaining motion.

What causes hallux rigidus?

Hallux rigidus develops from a combination of mechanical and structural factors that place extra stress on the big toe joint over time. Contributing factors include:

  • Biomechanical issues that alter how weight moves through the big toe joint
  • Fallen arches or flat feet — see our flat feet page for more on how arch position affects the rest of the foot
  • Ankle pronation (rolling inward)
  • Family history of hallux rigidus or similar joint conditions
  • Repetitive stress from certain occupations or activities
  • A prior injury to the big toe joint
  • Inflammatory diseases such as rheumatoid arthritis or gout, which can accelerate joint damage — learn more about rheumatoid arthritis and gout

What are the symptoms of hallux rigidus?

Symptoms tend to progress as the condition advances. In the early stages, you may notice:

  • Pain and stiffness in the big toe during walking, standing, or bending
  • Stiffness that worsens in cold or damp weather
  • Swelling around the big toe joint
  • Difficulty with activities that require pushing off the big toe, such as running or squatting

As hallux rigidus becomes more advanced, additional symptoms can develop, including:

  • Pain even at rest, without activity
  • Difficulty finding comfortable shoes because of bone spurs forming at the joint
  • A change in your walking pattern to avoid bending the toe, which can lead to secondary pain in the knee, hip, or lower back

How do we diagnose hallux rigidus?

Diagnosis is based on a physical examination that checks the range of motion in the big toe joint, along with a review of your symptoms and how they have progressed. X-rays are used to assess the severity of arthritis in the joint, identify any bone spurs, and help determine which stage of hallux rigidus you are experiencing — information that guides the right treatment approach.

How is hallux rigidus treated?

Treatment depends on the stage of the condition, with conservative options generally tried first:

Non-surgical treatment

  • Shoe modifications, such as a stiff sole or rocker-bottom design, to reduce the need to bend the toe
  • Custom orthotics to improve how weight moves through the foot — see our custom orthotics options
  • NSAIDs to help manage pain and inflammation
  • Corticosteroid injections for more significant flare-ups
  • Physical therapy to maintain joint mobility and reduce strain on surrounding structures

Surgery

When conservative treatments no longer provide adequate relief, surgery is the only way to eliminate or significantly reduce pain from hallux rigidus. The specific procedure recommended depends on how advanced the arthritis is, along with your age and activity level — options range from removing bone spurs and reshaping the joint to more involved joint-preserving or joint-replacing procedures. Your podiatrist will walk you through which approach fits your situation.

What is recovery from hallux rigidus treatment like?

Many people manage hallux rigidus for years with shoe modifications, orthotics, and periodic treatment for flare-ups. When surgery becomes necessary, recovery time varies depending on the specific procedure performed, ranging from a few weeks of protected weight-bearing for less invasive procedures to a longer recovery for more extensive joint surgery. Your podiatrist will give you a personalized recovery timeline based on your procedure.

When should you see a podiatrist for big toe pain?

See a podiatrist if your big toe is becoming stiffer, more painful, or harder to move over time, or if pain is starting to change the way you walk. Catching hallux rigidus in its earlier stages generally means more treatment options are available before the joint becomes severely restricted. When you are ready, you can request an appointment at either of our Northern Utah offices.

Common Questions

Hallux rigidus FAQs

Is hallux rigidus the same as a bunion?

No, though they affect the same joint and are sometimes confused. A bunion is a bony bump from a shift in joint alignment, while hallux rigidus is a degenerative, arthritic stiffening of the joint itself. It is possible to have both conditions.

What is the difference between hallux limitus and hallux rigidus?

Hallux limitus is the earlier stage, where the big toe joint has limited but not fully restricted motion. As the condition progresses to hallux rigidus, the joint becomes increasingly stiff and can eventually become what is often described as a "frozen joint."

What causes hallux rigidus?

Hallux rigidus develops from a combination of factors, including biomechanical issues, flat feet, ankle pronation, family history, repetitive stress from certain activities or occupations, a prior toe injury, or inflammatory conditions such as rheumatoid arthritis or gout.

Can hallux rigidus be treated without surgery?

Yes, especially in earlier stages. Shoe modifications, custom orthotics, NSAIDs, corticosteroid injections, and physical therapy can help manage pain and preserve function for years in many people. Surgery is considered when these options no longer provide adequate relief.

Will hallux rigidus get worse over time?

Hallux rigidus is a progressive, degenerative condition, so it can continue to worsen over time, though the rate of progression varies from person to person. Early treatment and shoe modifications can help slow symptoms and preserve joint function.

Do I need surgery for hallux rigidus?

Not everyone does. Many people manage symptoms for years with conservative care. Surgery becomes the recommended option when nonsurgical treatments no longer relieve pain, since surgery is the only way to eliminate or significantly reduce pain in more advanced cases.

When should I see a podiatrist for big toe stiffness?

See a podiatrist if your big toe is becoming increasingly stiff or painful, or if it is changing the way you walk. Earlier evaluation generally means more treatment options are available. You can request an appointment at our Farmington or South Ogden office.
