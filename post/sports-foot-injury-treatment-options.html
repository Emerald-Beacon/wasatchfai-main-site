Sports Foot Injury Treatment Options

Most sports injuries to the foot are very treatable with early intervention. Here's how to recognize what you're dealing with and get back to your sport the right way.

By the physicians at Wasatch Foot & Ankle Institute · Updated July 13, 2026

Most sports-related foot injuries are very treatable, especially when you act early instead of "playing through it." The four categories to know are sprains, strains, stress fractures, and tendon injuries — and each responds best to a specific, staged approach to care. Whether you're a weekend hiker, a competitive runner, or a weekend-warrior athlete in Farmington or South Ogden, understanding what you're dealing with helps you get back on your feet faster and reduces the risk of a repeat injury.

Foot and ankle injuries are among the most common reasons active patients visit our offices. Below, our podiatrists break down the injury types we see most often, what to do in the first 48 hours, and the full range of treatments — from home care to advanced in-office procedures.

What are the most common sports foot injuries?

Sports foot injuries generally fall into four categories:

  • Sprains — ligament injuries caused by rolling or twisting the ankle or foot
  • Strains — muscle or tendon injuries usually caused by overuse or sudden overload
  • Stress fractures — small cracks in bone caused by repetitive force, common in runners
  • Tendon problems — including Achilles tendonitis and other overuse tendinopathies

Each has a different healing timeline and treatment path, which is why an accurate diagnosis matters before you decide how quickly to return to activity.

What should I do right after a sports injury?

In the first 24 to 48 hours after an injury, remember R.I.C.E. — rest, ice, compression, and elevation. This combination limits swelling and controls the initial inflammatory response while you figure out how serious the injury is.

Just as important: don't ignore the pain. Continuing to run, jump, or play on an injured foot or ankle can turn a minor sprain or strain into a more serious tear or fracture. If pain, swelling, or instability doesn't meaningfully improve within a few days, it's time to have it evaluated.

How are sports foot injuries treated?

Treatment is staged to match the severity of the injury, starting conservative and escalating only if needed.

Physical therapy

Physical therapy is the most important step in the recovery process for most sports injuries. A structured program rebuilds strength, flexibility, and balance around the injured area — and it also helps correct the mechanical issues that may have contributed to the injury in the first place, lowering your risk of re-injury.

Immobilization

Walking boots or braces protect the injured foot or ankle while it heals, allowing you to stay mobile without placing full stress on healing tissue.

Injections and shockwave therapy

For persistent pain or slow-healing tendon injuries, corticosteroid injections can calm inflammation, and shockwave therapy uses focused sound-wave energy to stimulate the body's natural healing response without surgery.

Surgery

Surgery is reserved for the more severe cases — complete ligament or tendon tears, or unstable fractures that won't heal properly on their own. Our physicians always start with the least invasive option that gives you a good outcome.

What else supports recovery?

Alongside formal treatment, a few supportive habits can help your body heal:

  • An anti-inflammatory diet, with foods like leafy greens, berries, and fatty fish
  • Fish oil supplements, which some patients use to help manage inflammation (talk with your physician first)
  • Low-impact cross-training, such as swimming or cycling, to maintain fitness while protecting the injured area

How can I prevent sports foot injuries?

Prevention matters just as much as treatment. A few habits go a long way toward keeping you in the game:

  • Wear proper, sport-specific footwear and replace worn-out shoes
  • Use custom orthotics if you have flat feet, high arches, or a history of injury
  • Warm up and stretch adequately before activity
  • Listen to your body — soreness that lingers or worsens is a signal to slow down

If you've sustained a foot or ankle injury that isn't improving, or if you're not sure how serious it is, our physicians can evaluate it and get you on the right recovery path. You can request an appointment at either of our Northern Utah offices.

Common Questions

Sports foot injury FAQs

What's the difference between a sprain and a strain?

A sprain is an injury to a ligament, the tissue connecting bone to bone, usually caused by rolling or twisting the ankle or foot. A strain is an injury to a muscle or tendon, typically from overuse or sudden overload. Both can cause pain and swelling, but they're treated somewhat differently.

Can I keep playing on a minor foot injury?

It's not a good idea. Continuing to play through pain can turn a minor sprain or strain into a more serious tear or fracture. If you have pain, swelling, or instability after an injury, it's safer to rest and have it evaluated before returning to your sport.

How long does it take to recover from a sports foot injury?

Recovery time depends on the type and severity of the injury. Mild sprains and strains may improve within a couple of weeks with R.I.C.E. and physical therapy, while stress fractures and more severe ligament or tendon injuries can take several weeks to months to fully heal.

Do I need physical therapy for a sports foot injury?

For most sports injuries, physical therapy is considered the most important step in recovery. It rebuilds strength and flexibility around the injured area and helps correct mechanical issues that may have contributed to the injury, reducing your risk of re-injury.

When does a sports foot injury need surgery?

Surgery is reserved for more severe injuries, such as complete ligament or tendon tears or unstable fractures that won't heal properly with conservative care. Most sports foot injuries improve without surgery when treated early and appropriately.

How can I prevent future sports foot injuries?

Wear proper, sport-specific footwear, consider custom orthotics if you have flat feet or a history of injury, warm up and stretch before activity, and pay attention to soreness that lingers or worsens. These habits significantly lower your risk of re-injury.
