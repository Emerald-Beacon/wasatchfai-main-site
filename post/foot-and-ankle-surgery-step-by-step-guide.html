Foot and Ankle Surgery: A Step-by-Step Guide

Surgery can feel like the biggest unknown in your treatment plan. Here's a clear, step-by-step look at what actually happens — before, during, and after.

By the physicians at Wasatch Foot & Ankle Institute · Updated July 13, 2026

Foot and ankle surgery is usually the last step after conservative treatments haven't resolved a problem — and understanding what actually happens, from consultation to recovery, can make the process far less intimidating. Whether you're facing a bunion correction, fracture repair, or a minimally invasive tendon procedure, most surgeries follow a similar general path.

Our surgeons walk every patient through their specific procedure in detail, but here's a general, step-by-step look at what to expect if surgery is part of your treatment plan.

Step 1: Consultation and diagnosis

Before surgery is ever discussed, your podiatrist will examine your foot or ankle, review your symptoms and history, and typically order imaging such as X-rays or, for soft-tissue issues, an MRI or ultrasound. Surgery is recommended only after conservative treatments — rest, bracing, physical therapy, orthotics, or injections — have been tried and haven't resolved the problem, or when the injury itself requires structural repair.

Step 2: Pre-operative planning

Once surgery is planned, you'll have a pre-op appointment to review the procedure, discuss anesthesia options, and go over instructions for the days leading up to surgery. This may include fasting requirements, adjusting certain medications, and arranging transportation, since you won't be able to drive yourself home. This is also the time to ask questions about recovery timelines, activity restrictions, and what your specific procedure involves.

Step 3: The day of surgery

Many foot and ankle procedures are done on an outpatient basis, meaning you go home the same day. Depending on the procedure, anesthesia may range from local numbing and conscious sedation for minimally invasive, in-office treatments to general or regional anesthesia for more involved surgery performed in a surgical center. Your care team will monitor you throughout the procedure and in a recovery area afterward before you're discharged.

Step 4: The first days after surgery

Immediately after surgery, the focus is on protecting the surgical site and managing swelling and discomfort. Most patients are sent home with a dressing, splint, boot, or cast, and specific instructions on weight-bearing — some procedures require staying completely off the foot, while others allow limited weight-bearing right away. Elevating the foot above heart level and icing as directed helps control swelling in this early period.

Step 5: Follow-up visits and healing

You'll return for follow-up visits so your surgeon can check the incision, monitor healing on X-rays if needed, and adjust your weight-bearing status as you progress. Dressings or sutures are typically addressed at these visits. This is also when your care team will tell you when it's safe to transition out of a boot or cast and into supportive footwear.

Step 6: Rehabilitation and return to activity

Many patients benefit from physical therapy to rebuild strength, flexibility, and balance once the surgical site has healed enough to tolerate movement. Returning to full activity — walking, exercise, or sport — happens gradually and is guided by your surgeon based on how your specific procedure is healing, not a fixed calendar date. Rushing this stage is one of the most common reasons for setbacks, so following your care team's pace matters.

Questions before you decide

If surgery has been recommended, it's reasonable to ask about the specific goals of the procedure, what nonsurgical options were considered, expected recovery time, and what daily life will look like during recovery. Our physicians are happy to walk through all of this so you can make an informed decision. You can request an appointment to discuss your options at either of our Northern Utah offices.

Common Questions

Foot & ankle surgery FAQs

Is foot and ankle surgery always the first option?

No. Our podiatrists exhaust conservative treatments — rest, bracing, physical therapy, orthotics, and injections — before recommending surgery. Surgery is generally reserved for cases that haven't responded to nonsurgical care or injuries that require structural repair, such as certain fractures.

What kind of anesthesia is used for foot and ankle surgery?

It depends on the procedure. Many in-office and minimally invasive procedures use local anesthesia or conscious sedation, which keeps you comfortable and relaxed without a full general anesthetic. More involved procedures may be done in a surgical center with general or regional anesthesia. Your surgeon will discuss the right option for your case.

How long does it take to recover from foot or ankle surgery?

Recovery time varies widely depending on the procedure, from a few weeks for minimally invasive treatments to several months for reconstructive surgery or fracture repair. Your surgeon will give you a personalized timeline based on your specific procedure and health history.

Will I need to be non-weight-bearing after surgery?

Many foot and ankle procedures require a period of limited or no weight-bearing to protect the healing bone or soft tissue, often using a boot, cast, or crutches. Your surgical team will tell you exactly how much weight you can put on the foot and when that changes, and following those instructions closely helps prevent complications.

What can I do to prepare for foot or ankle surgery?

Follow your pre-op instructions closely, including any fasting requirements, medication adjustments, and arranging a ride home. It also helps to prepare your home in advance — set up a comfortable resting area, stock up on ice packs, and arrange help for the first few days when mobility is most limited.
