Why Your Arch Pain Keeps Coming Back

If the ache in your arch fades for a while and then returns, the problem probably isn't gone — it's just been masked. Here's what's really going on.

By the physicians at Wasatch Foot & Ankle Institute · Updated July 13, 2026

Recurring arch pain almost always means the underlying cause was never fully treated — only quieted for a while. Your arch is a load-bearing structure made of bones, ligaments, tendons, and muscles that supports your body weight and absorbs shock with every step. When something in that system is out of balance — inflamed tissue, a structural issue, unsupportive shoes, or too much activity too soon — rest alone often brings temporary relief without resolving what caused the pain in the first place.

If you've noticed a pattern of arch pain flaring up, easing off, and then returning again a few weeks or months later, you're describing a very common cycle we see in the clinic. Below, our podiatrists break down the most common reasons arch pain keeps coming back and what it actually takes to break the cycle for good.

What is arch pain, exactly?

Your foot's arch is supported by a network of bones, ligaments, tendons, and muscles working together to bear your body weight and cushion each step. Arch pain can range from a dull ache after a long day on your feet to a sharp, localized pain that limits how you walk. Because so many structures contribute to the arch, pinpointing the exact cause is the key to lasting relief.

Is plantar fasciitis the reason my arch pain keeps returning?

Plantar fasciitis is one of the most common causes of recurring arch and heel pain, particularly in people with flat feet or high arches. It develops when the plantar fascia — the band of tissue running along the bottom of the foot — becomes irritated and inflamed from repeated strain. If you rest until the pain fades but never address the underlying strain, it's very common for the same irritation to flare back up once you resume normal activity.

Could my shoes be causing the pain to keep returning?

Footwear without proper arch support is a frequent culprit. High heels, flip-flops, and worn-out sneakers all fail to give the arch the structure it needs, which leaves the plantar fascia and surrounding muscles doing extra work with every step. If you treat a flare-up but then go right back to the same unsupportive shoes, the pain has every reason to come back.

Can doing too much too soon bring the pain back?

Yes. A sudden increase in activity — a new running plan, more time on your feet at work, or a return to sport after time off — can overload the arch before it has adapted. This is a common reason arch pain resolves with rest, only to reappear once activity ramps back up. Gradually increasing intensity gives the tissues time to strengthen and adjust.

Does foot structure play a role in recurring arch pain?

Flat feet, high arches, and abnormal pronation patterns all change how weight moves through the foot with each step. These structural factors don't go away on their own, which means arch pain tied to them tends to return again and again unless the underlying mechanics are addressed — typically with custom orthotics, targeted physical therapy, or supportive footwear built for your specific foot shape.

Why does masking the symptoms make arch pain keep coming back?

Over-the-counter pain relievers, rest, and ice can all ease arch pain temporarily, but they don't correct what caused it. Without a proper evaluation and treatment plan, the same triggers — poor footwear, unsupported foot structure, overuse — remain in place, so the pain is likely to resurface. Breaking the cycle usually requires identifying and treating the root cause rather than just calming the symptoms each time they appear.

How is recurring arch pain treated?

Treatment starts with pinpointing the cause. From there, a typical plan may include:

  • Switching to supportive, well-fitted footwear
  • Custom orthotics to correct how weight loads through the arch — see our custom orthotics options
  • Physical therapy and targeted stretching for the plantar fascia and surrounding muscles
  • Gradually adjusting activity levels rather than resuming full intensity all at once
  • Consistently following your treatment plan rather than stopping once symptoms ease

Consulting a foot specialist for a proper diagnosis is the most reliable way to stop the cycle rather than treating each flare-up as it happens.

When should I see a podiatrist for arch pain?

See a podiatrist if your arch pain has returned more than once, lasts longer than a week or two each time, or is starting to limit your normal activities. You can request an appointment at either of our Northern Utah offices to get a full evaluation and a plan built for your feet.

Common Questions

Recurring arch pain FAQs

Why does my arch pain keep coming back after resting?

Rest often eases the inflammation causing arch pain, but it doesn't fix the underlying trigger — whether that's unsupportive shoes, foot structure, or overuse. Once you resume normal activity, the same trigger is still there, so the pain tends to return.

Is plantar fasciitis the most common cause of recurring arch pain?

It's one of the most common causes we see, especially in people with flat feet or high arches. Plantar fasciitis develops from repeated strain on the tissue along the bottom of the foot, and it can keep flaring up if the underlying strain isn't addressed.

Can the wrong shoes really cause arch pain to return?

Yes. Shoes without arch support — like high heels, flip-flops, or worn-out sneakers — leave the plantar fascia and surrounding muscles doing extra work with every step, which can bring pain back even after it seems resolved.

Do flat feet or high arches make arch pain more likely to recur?

Yes. Structural factors like flat feet, high arches, or abnormal pronation change how weight moves through the foot. These don't resolve on their own, so pain tied to them tends to return unless the underlying mechanics are corrected with orthotics, footwear, or therapy.

Should I just keep icing and resting when my arch pain comes back?

Icing and rest can ease a flare-up, but repeatedly masking symptoms without treating the cause usually means the pain keeps returning. A proper evaluation helps identify what's actually driving the pain so it can be resolved rather than managed over and over.

When should I see a podiatrist about arch pain?

See a podiatrist if your arch pain has returned more than once, lasts longer than a week or two, or is starting to limit your activities. You can request an appointment at our Farmington or South Ogden office for a full evaluation.
