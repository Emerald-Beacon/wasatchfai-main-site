Simple Solutions to Relieve Arthritis Pain With Easy Exercises

Arthritis in the feet doesn't have to sideline you. A few minutes of daily stretching, the right footwear, and some smart habits can meaningfully ease pain and improve mobility.

By the physicians at Wasatch Foot & Ankle Institute · Updated July 13, 2026

Arthritis pain in the feet responds well to a combination of simple, consistent habits: daily stretching, supportive footwear, and short low-impact exercises that keep the joints moving. You don't need special equipment or a long routine — just a few minutes a day, done consistently, can noticeably ease stiffness and improve how your feet feel with every step.

Arthritis is one of the most common reasons people lose mobility and comfort as they get older, and it commonly shows up first in the feet, which absorb impact with every step you take. Whether your arthritis comes from osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, gout, or an old injury that never quite healed right, the same foundational habits can help. Below, our podiatrists walk through easy exercises, footwear tips, and recovery habits that make a real difference.

What causes arthritis pain in the feet?

Arthritis pain can develop from several different underlying conditions, including osteoarthritis (wear-and-tear on the joint cartilage), rheumatoid arthritis (an autoimmune condition), gout (caused by uric acid crystals in the joint), or old injuries that were never fully rehabilitated. Decades of repetitive motion, untreated injuries, or a general lack of joint care can eventually catch up with your feet, since they carry your full body weight through 26 bones and more than 30 joints on each side.

Why does proper footwear make such a difference?

Unsupportive or worn-out shoes increase pressure on already sensitive, arthritic joints with every step. Investing in supportive shoes that fit well — with a wide toe box, good arch support, and adequate cushioning — can reduce arthritis pain significantly. If off-the-shelf shoes and inserts aren't cutting it, a podiatrist can recommend custom orthotics tailored to your specific foot shape and the joints that need the most support.

Why does stretching help arthritis pain?

Gentle, consistent stretching encourages blood flow to the joints and helps reduce the stiffness that makes arthritis pain worse. Regular stretching — even just a few minutes a day — can decrease overall pain levels and improve mobility over time, especially when it's paired with supportive footwear and the other habits below.

What are some easy exercises for arthritis relief?

These simple, low-impact movements can be done seated at home and are a good starting point for most people with foot or ankle arthritis:

  • Ankle circles: Rotate each ankle slowly, 10 times in each direction
  • Toe flex and stretch: Curl your toes tightly, then extend them fully, holding each position for about 5 seconds
  • Seated heel raises: Press your toes into the floor, lift your heels, hold for 5 seconds, and repeat 10 times
  • Gentle walking: Maintain a steady, comfortable pace for short intervals to keep joints moving without overloading them

Check with your podiatrist before starting a new exercise routine if you have significant pain, swelling, or a joint condition that hasn't been evaluated.

How important is rest and recovery?

Rest is just as important as movement when it comes to managing arthritis. Warm compresses, gentle massage, or rolling the bottom of your foot over a massage ball can help release stiffness and improve circulation after activity. Supportive tools like cushioned insoles, braces, and massage tools can also help absorb shock and stabilize the joint between exercise sessions.

What daily habits help prevent arthritis flare-ups?

Consistency matters more than intensity. Building a few minutes of stretching and a short walk into your daily routine, checking in on your joints regularly, and replacing worn-out footwear promptly all help prevent flare-ups before they start. Small, sustainable habits add up to meaningfully better joint health over time.

When should I see a specialist for arthritis pain?

See a podiatrist, rheumatologist, or orthopedic doctor if you notice pain that interferes with sleep or daily activities, swelling or warmth in a joint, significant stiffness, or a reduced ability to walk or bend comfortably. Arthritis may be common, but it should never be treated as an unavoidable part of aging — with consistency, patience, and the right professional guidance, long-term relief is achievable. Common Questions

Arthritis pain relief FAQs

What is the fastest way to relieve arthritis pain at home?

Applying heat, doing gentle stretches, or rolling your foot over a warm massage ball can reduce stiffness relatively quickly. These measures work best as part of a consistent routine rather than a one-time fix.

Can exercises really help arthritis?

Yes, exercise is one of the most effective ways to manage arthritis symptoms. Gentle stretching and low-impact movement help preserve mobility, reduce stiffness, and keep the surrounding muscles supporting the joint.

How do I know if my arthritis is getting worse?

Increased stiffness, new or worsening swelling, or a noticeable decrease in comfort or mobility are all signs it may be time for a medical evaluation. Don't wait for pain to become severe before checking in with a specialist.

Are custom orthotics worth it for arthritis?

For many people, yes. Custom orthotics are built to your specific foot shape and can meaningfully reduce joint stress, improve alignment, and provide targeted cushioning for longer-term relief compared to generic inserts.

Who should I see for chronic arthritis pain in my feet?

Podiatrists specialize in foot and ankle joints and can provide targeted care, including footwear guidance, orthotics, and in-office treatments. Rheumatologists manage the underlying disease process for conditions like rheumatoid arthritis, and the two often work together.
