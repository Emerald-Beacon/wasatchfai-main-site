Walking on Clouds: The Importance of Proper Footwear

Improper footwear is one of the most common causes of foot pain we see in clinic. Here's how the right shoes protect your feet, your posture, and your performance — and how to choose them.

By the physicians at Wasatch Foot & Ankle Institute · Updated July 13, 2026

Proper footwear is one of the simplest, most overlooked ways to protect your feet, ankles, and the joints above them. Shoes that fit well, cushion each step, and support your arch reduce your risk of injury and can ease pain that starts in your feet but radiates into your knees, hips, and back. Shoes that don't do those things are one of the most common causes of foot pain we see in clinic.

It's easy to think of shoes as an afterthought — something you grab on your way out the door. But your feet absorb thousands of steps of impact every day, and the shoes you choose determine how well they hold up to that load. Below, we break down what "proper footwear" actually means and how to shop for it.

What makes footwear "proper"?

Proper footwear provides adequate support, cushioning, and protection for your feet. That means a fit that matches the true shape and size of your foot, quality materials that hold their support over time, and a sole built for the activity you're doing in them. A shoe that looks good but doesn't do these things isn't doing its job, no matter how new it is.

Why the right shoes matter

Injury prevention

Well-fitted, supportive shoes reduce the risk of sprains, strains, and other foot and ankle injuries during running, walking, or long periods on your feet. A shoe with poor traction, worn-out cushioning, or no lateral support gives your ankle less to work with the moment you step wrong.

Better posture

Your feet are the foundation for the rest of your body. Shoes that properly support your feet and ankles can help reduce strain that would otherwise travel up into your knees, hips, and back. If you've ever dealt with unexplained back or knee pain, your footwear is worth a look.

Everyday comfort

Feet that are well-supported throughout the day experience less pain, swelling, and fatigue by evening. This matters most for people who spend long stretches standing or walking — retail and healthcare workers, teachers, and anyone who's on their feet for a living.

Athletic performance

The right shoe for your sport or activity improves balance, stability, and agility, whether you're a competitive athlete or simply enjoy recreational activity on the weekends. Running shoes, hiking boots, and court shoes are each built differently for a reason.

How to choose the right shoes

  • Get measured regularly. Foot size and shape change over time — including width, arch height, and length — so don't assume your size from ten years ago is still accurate. Have your feet professionally measured periodically, especially if you've noticed new discomfort.
  • Prioritize quality over price. Durable, well-constructed materials cost more up front but hold their support and cushioning longer, which matters more for your feet than the sticker price.
  • Match the shoe to the activity. A shoe built for running isn't built for standing all day on concrete, and neither is built for hiking uneven trails. Choose footwear designed for what you'll actually be doing in it.
  • Check the fit at the end of the day. Feet swell slightly through the day, so shopping in the afternoon or evening gives you a more accurate fit.
  • Don't ignore existing foot conditions. If you have flat feet, plantar fasciitis, or another diagnosed condition, ask your podiatrist what features to look for — or whether custom orthotics would help.

When footwear alone isn't enough

Even the right shoes can't fix every foot problem on their own. If you're dealing with persistent pain, numbness, swelling, or a foot shape that makes off-the-shelf shoes uncomfortable no matter what you try, it's worth having a podiatrist take a look. We can identify what's actually driving your discomfort and recommend footwear, orthotics, or treatment specific to your feet. When you're ready, you can request an appointment at either of our Northern Utah offices.

Common Questions

Proper footwear FAQs

How often should I get my feet measured for shoes?

It's a good idea to have your feet professionally measured every couple of years, or sooner if you've noticed new discomfort. Foot size and shape can change over time due to age, weight changes, pregnancy, or existing foot conditions.

Can the wrong shoes actually cause back or knee pain?

Yes. Your feet are the foundation for the rest of your body, so poor arch support or cushioning can change how you walk and place added strain on your knees, hips, and back. Supportive footwear is often part of managing those symptoms.

What's the best time of day to shop for shoes?

Later in the day is usually best, since feet swell slightly with activity. Shopping in the afternoon or evening gives you a more accurate sense of how a shoe will fit during normal daily wear.

Do I need different shoes for different activities?

Yes. Shoes designed for running, standing all day, hiking, or court sports are each built with different support, cushioning, and traction. Wearing a shoe outside its intended use increases your risk of discomfort or injury.

Should I see a podiatrist before buying orthotics or special shoes?

If you have a diagnosed foot condition, persistent pain, or a foot shape that off-the-shelf shoes don't accommodate well, seeing a podiatrist first can help you avoid guessing. We can recommend specific footwear features or custom orthotics based on your feet.

What are signs my current shoes aren't supportive enough?

Common signs include foot, heel, or arch pain that worsens through the day, visible wear on the sole or heel, feeling unstable on uneven ground, or new knee, hip, or back discomfort. If these sound familiar, it may be time for new shoes or a podiatry evaluation.
