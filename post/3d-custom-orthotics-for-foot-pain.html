3D Custom Orthotics for Foot Pain

Off-the-shelf inserts treat every foot the same. 3D custom orthotics are built from a precise scan of your foot to treat yours.

By the physicians at Wasatch Foot & Ankle Institute · Updated July 13, 2026

3D custom orthotics are shoe inserts built from a precise digital scan of your own foot, designed to correct the specific alignment and pressure patterns causing your pain. Because they are made to match your foot's exact contours and how you walk, they typically relieve chronic foot pain more effectively than generic over-the-counter insoles.

Chronic foot pain rarely has one single cause — it's usually the result of how your particular foot is shaped and how it moves under load. That's why a one-size-fits-all insole from the drugstore often helps for a while and then stops working, or never quite fits right in the first place. 3D custom orthotics take a different approach: rather than guessing at support, they're engineered around a digital map of your foot.

What conditions do custom orthotics treat?

Custom orthotics are commonly recommended for foot pain linked to structural or biomechanical issues, including:

  • Plantar fasciitis and chronic heel pain
  • Flat feet and fallen arches
  • Overpronation (the foot rolling inward excessively when walking)
  • High, rigid arches that don't absorb shock well
  • Arthritis affecting the foot or ankle joints
  • Tendon inflammation, including posterior tibial tendon dysfunction

Left untreated, these conditions often become chronic, since the underlying mechanical problem keeps re-irritating the same tissue with every step.

What makes 3D custom orthotics different from insoles you buy off the shelf?

3D custom orthotics are more than upgraded insoles. They're created through a detailed fitting process using high-precision 3D scanning to capture the exact contours of your feet — the height of your arch, the way your weight distributes across the sole, and the subtle asymmetries between your left and right foot. A generic insert can only approximate an average foot shape; a custom orthotic is built around yours specifically.

That precision lets the orthotic address the specific issue driving your pain — offloading pressure from an overworked area, correcting overpronation, or supporting a high arch that isn't absorbing shock the way it should — rather than offering generalized cushioning.

What does the fitting process involve?

The process starts with an in-person evaluation. A podiatrist examines your feet, assesses your gait, and identifies the biomechanical issue behind your symptoms. From there, a digital 3D scan captures precise measurements of your foot's shape and pressure distribution. Those measurements are used to design and manufacture an orthotic tailored specifically to your feet, which is then fitted and adjusted to your shoes.

How long do custom orthotics last?

Most custom orthotics last one to three years, depending on your activity level, body weight, and how well you care for them. Athletes and people who are on their feet all day tend to wear through the supportive materials faster than someone with a more sedentary routine. Your podiatrist can tell you what to watch for — flattened support, visible wear, or a return of your original symptoms — as signs it's time for a replacement.

Are custom orthotics an alternative to surgery?

For many patients, yes. Because custom orthotics correct the underlying mechanical stress causing pain, they can reduce or eliminate symptoms without surgery or long-term reliance on medication. They're often one part of a broader conservative treatment plan that may also include stretching, activity modification, and supportive footwear. Surgery is generally reserved for cases that don't respond to these conservative measures.

If you've been dealing with foot pain that doesn't improve with store-bought inserts, it may be time for an evaluation. You can request an appointment at our Farmington or South Ogden office to find out whether custom orthotics are right for you.

Common Questions

Custom orthotics FAQs

How are 3D custom orthotics different from insoles I can buy at a store?

Store-bought insoles are made to a generic foot shape, while 3D custom orthotics are built from a precise digital scan of your own feet. That means they're designed to correct the specific alignment or pressure issue causing your pain, rather than offering general cushioning.

What conditions can custom orthotics help with?

Custom orthotics are commonly used for plantar fasciitis, flat feet, overpronation, high arches, arthritis, and tendon inflammation. They work by correcting the underlying mechanical stress that's driving the pain.

What happens during the orthotic fitting process?

A podiatrist first evaluates your feet and gait to identify the issue behind your symptoms. Then a digital 3D scan captures precise measurements of your foot's shape and pressure distribution, which are used to design and manufacture an orthotic built specifically for you.

How long do custom orthotics last?

Most custom orthotics last one to three years, depending on activity level and how well they're cared for. Athletes and people on their feet all day may need to replace them sooner than someone with a more sedentary routine.

Can custom orthotics help me avoid foot surgery?

For many patients, yes. Because they correct the mechanical stress causing pain, custom orthotics can reduce or eliminate symptoms without surgery. They're often used as part of a broader conservative treatment plan, with surgery reserved for cases that don't respond to conservative care.

Are custom orthotics covered by insurance?

Coverage varies by plan and by the medical necessity of the orthotic. Our office can help verify your benefits before you commit to treatment — request an appointment and our team will walk you through your options.
