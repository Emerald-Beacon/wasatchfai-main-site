How to Prepare for Ankle Surgery

A little preparation before ankle surgery — the right questions, a recovery-ready home, and some prehab work — can make your procedure and recovery go far more smoothly.

By the physicians at Wasatch Foot & Ankle Institute · Updated July 13, 2026

Preparing for ankle surgery involves more than showing up on the day of your procedure. Choosing the right surgeon, setting up your home for recovery, doing some pre-surgery strengthening, and planning for the weeks afterward all help your surgery go smoothly and your recovery move forward on schedule. Whether you are having surgery for a fracture, chronic instability, or advanced arthritis, a little planning ahead goes a long way.

Our surgeons in Farmington and South Ogden walk every patient through what to expect before and after ankle surgery. Here is the same guidance we share in the exam room, organized into a practical checklist you can start working through as soon as surgery is on the calendar.

Choosing the right surgeon

Your outcome starts with the surgeon and surgical plan. When you meet with your podiatric surgeon, it is reasonable to ask about their experience with your specific condition, what surgical approach they recommend — arthroscopic (minimally invasive) versus open surgery — and what a realistic recovery timeline looks like for your situation. A good surgeon will walk you through the risks, benefits, and alternatives so you can make an informed decision.

Getting your home ready before surgery

You will likely have limited mobility for at least the first few weeks, so setting up your space in advance makes daily life much easier. Before your surgery date:

  • Clear walkways of rugs, cords, and clutter that could cause a fall
  • Set up a recovery station with your phone, water, medications, and remote within reach
  • Consider a shower chair and, if needed, a raised toilet seat
  • Stock the kitchen and freezer with easy, prepared meals
  • Move frequently used items to waist height so you don't need to bend or reach

Strengthening before surgery (prehabilitation)

"Prehab" — light exercises done in the weeks before surgery — can help you go into your procedure stronger, which often supports an easier recovery. Ask your surgeon or physical therapist whether gentle ankle circles, calf raises, and resistance band work are appropriate for your condition before your surgery date. Not every patient is a candidate for prehab, so check with your care team before starting any new exercise.

The mental side of surgery

Feeling nervous before surgery is common and completely normal. Many patients find it helpful to write out their questions in advance, ask their care team to walk through what the day will look like step by step, and talk openly with family or friends about their concerns. Knowing what to expect tends to ease anxiety more than anything else.

Surgery day logistics

Arrange for someone to drive you home, since you will not be able to drive after anesthesia. Wear loose, comfortable clothing that is easy to get on and off over a bandaged foot, and ask your surgical team ahead of time about anesthesia options, fasting instructions, and what to bring with you.

Planning your recovery

Recovery typically involves a combination of pain management, ice, elevation, and rest in the first days after surgery, followed by physical therapy once your surgeon clears you to begin. Recovery timelines vary widely depending on the procedure, but many patients are progressing through structured rehab within a few weeks and continuing to improve for several months. Following your surgeon's weight-bearing and activity restrictions closely is one of the most important things you can do for a smooth recovery — see our general recovery guidance for more.

When to talk to a podiatric surgeon

If you have an ankle injury or condition that has not improved with conservative care, or if a surgeon has recommended surgery and you would like a thorough consultation, our team can walk you through your options and what to expect. You can request an appointment at either of our Northern Utah offices.

Common Questions

Ankle surgery FAQs

How long does recovery from ankle surgery take?

Recovery timelines vary by procedure and individual, but many patients progress through structured rehabilitation over roughly six to twelve weeks, with continued improvement for several months afterward. Your surgeon can give you a timeline specific to your procedure.

What questions should I ask my ankle surgeon?

Ask about their experience with your specific condition, whether they recommend an arthroscopic or open approach, expected recovery time, and what results are realistic for your case. A good surgeon will walk through the risks, benefits, and alternatives with you.

Should I do exercises before ankle surgery?

Many surgeons recommend light "prehab" exercises, such as ankle circles, calf raises, or resistance band work, in the weeks before surgery to help you go in stronger. Always check with your surgeon or physical therapist first, since not every patient or condition is a candidate for prehab.

How should I prepare my home for ankle surgery recovery?

Clear walkways of trip hazards, set up a recovery station with essentials within reach, consider a shower chair, and stock your kitchen with easy meals ahead of time. Moving frequently used items to waist height also helps you avoid unnecessary bending or reaching.

Will I be able to drive after ankle surgery?

You will not be able to drive immediately after surgery due to anesthesia, and you will need a ride home. Depending on which ankle was operated on and how your recovery progresses, your surgeon will let you know when it is safe to resume driving.

When should I talk to a surgeon about ankle surgery?

If an ankle injury or condition hasn't improved with conservative treatment, or a provider has already recommended surgery, it's a good time for a thorough consultation. You can request an appointment at our Farmington or South Ogden office.
