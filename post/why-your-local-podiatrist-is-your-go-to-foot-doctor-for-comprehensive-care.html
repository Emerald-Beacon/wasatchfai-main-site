Why Your Local Podiatrist Is Your Go-To Foot Doctor for Comprehensive Care

Your feet are the foundation of your body, carrying you through life's many journeys. Here's the full scope of care a local podiatrist provides — and why it goes far beyond treating pain when it shows up.

By the physicians at Wasatch Foot & Ankle Institute · Updated July 13, 2026

A podiatrist is a physician who specializes exclusively in the feet and ankles, which means they diagnose and treat conditions that a general practitioner may only see occasionally. That specialization covers far more than bunions and ingrown toenails — it spans diabetic foot care, sports injuries, pediatric foot development, and surgical treatment for severe conditions, all under one roof.

Because your feet absorb impact and support your full body weight with every step, small problems can compound quickly if they go unaddressed. A local podiatrist you see regularly is positioned to catch those problems early, before they become the kind of issue that limits your mobility or requires a much more involved treatment plan.

What does a podiatrist actually treat?

Podiatric care covers a wide range of conditions across every stage of life. Some of the most common areas of focus include:

Diabetic foot care

Diabetes can reduce circulation and sensation in the feet, which makes small cuts, blisters, or pressure points far more dangerous than they would be otherwise. Regular podiatric examinations catch these issues early — often before a patient can feel them — and are one of the most effective ways to prevent complications, including infections that can become serious if left untreated. Learn more on our diabetic foot care page.

Sports injuries

Sprains, stress fractures, and tendonitis are common among athletes and active adults alike. A podiatrist can accurately diagnose the specific structure involved — whether that's a ligament, tendon, or bone — and build a treatment plan that gets you back to activity safely, rather than guessing at a generic rest-and-ice approach.

Pediatric foot care

Children's feet are still developing, and issues like in-toeing, flat feet, or heel pain from growth plates need a different evaluation approach than an adult foot. Catching developmental concerns early can prevent them from becoming lifelong sources of discomfort.

Foot and ankle surgery

When conservative treatment isn't enough — for severe bunions, fractures, or advanced arthritis, for example — a podiatrist can perform surgical correction. Because they specialize in this anatomy specifically, they're able to offer surgical options that a general orthopedic practice may not routinely perform.

Why regular visits matter, not just emergency ones

It's easy to think of a podiatrist as someone you see only after something goes wrong. In practice, regular visits to your podiatry clinic are vital for maintaining foot health over the long term. Routine exams help prevent common issues — like ingrown nails, calluses, or early diabetic complications — and catch developing problems while they're still simple to treat.

This is especially true if you have diabetes, are highly active, are on your feet for work, or have a family history of foot and ankle conditions like hallux rigidus or flat feet. A relationship with a local podiatrist means someone who knows your history is evaluating changes over time, rather than seeing your foot for the first time in an urgent situation.

What if you need care right away?

Not every foot or ankle problem can wait for a routine visit. Same-day emergency appointments are available for issues like ingrown toenails, sprains, and breaks, so you're not stuck choosing between an urgent care visit with a generalist or waiting days to see a specialist.

Common Questions

Choosing a podiatrist FAQs

What's the difference between a podiatrist and my regular doctor?

A podiatrist specializes exclusively in the feet and ankles, with training focused on the specific bones, joints, tendons, and skin conditions unique to this part of the body. A general practitioner can handle routine concerns, but a podiatrist has deeper expertise for anything beyond the basics.

How often should I see a podiatrist?

For most healthy adults, an annual exam is a reasonable baseline. If you have diabetes, are highly active, or have an ongoing foot or ankle condition, more frequent visits — sometimes every few months — help catch problems before they become serious.

Do podiatrists treat children?

Yes. Podiatrists evaluate developmental concerns in children's feet, such as in-toeing, flat feet, or growth-related heel pain, using an approach tailored to a still-developing foot rather than a fully mature one.

Can a podiatrist help with diabetic foot problems?

Yes — diabetic foot care is one of the most important services a podiatrist provides. Regular exams catch reduced circulation, nerve changes, and small skin issues early, before they progress into more serious complications.

Is surgery always required for foot and ankle problems?

No. Most foot and ankle conditions are treated with conservative, nonsurgical care first. Surgery is reserved for cases that don't improve with those measures, or for structural problems — like severe bunions or fractures — that need it from the start.

Can I get a same-day appointment for a foot injury?

Yes. Same-day emergency appointments are available for issues like ingrown toenails, sprains, and breaks. You can request an appointment at our Farmington or South Ogden office.
