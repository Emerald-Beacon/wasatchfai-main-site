Toe Nail Fungus Removal Options

Thick, discolored, or crumbly toenails don't fix themselves. Here's how topical, oral, laser, and surgical treatments compare — and how to choose the right one.

By the physicians at Wasatch Foot & Ankle Institute · Updated July 13, 2026

Toenail fungus, known medically as onychomycosis, causes nails to thicken, discolor, and become brittle or crumbly over time. It doesn't go away on its own, and left untreated it can spread to other nails or the surrounding skin. The good news is there are several proven treatment paths, and the right one for you depends on how severe the infection is and how quickly you want results.

Below, we break down the main removal and treatment options — topical, oral, laser, and surgical — along with what to expect from each, and how to keep the fungus from coming back once it's gone.

What is toenail fungus?

Onychomycosis is a fungal infection of the nail plate or the nail bed underneath it. It thrives in warm, moist environments, which is why it's so common in people who wear closed shoes for long periods, spend time in locker rooms or pools, or have had an injured or previously infected nail. Signs include a nail that looks yellow, white, or brown; is thicker than normal; becomes brittle or crumbly at the edge; or separates from the nail bed.

Topical treatments

Topical antifungal creams and medicated nail lacquers — containing ingredients like ciclopirox or efinaconazole — are applied directly to the nail, usually once a day. They tend to work best for mild to moderate infections that haven't spread deep into the nail bed. Because the medication has to reach the infection as the nail grows out, topical treatment typically takes several months of consistent, daily use before you see a clear nail.

Oral antifungal medications

For moderate to severe infections, oral antifungals such as terbinafine or itraconazole treat the fungus systemically, through the bloodstream, which generally makes them more effective than topical treatment alone. A typical course runs anywhere from a few weeks to a few months. Because oral antifungals are processed by the liver, your podiatrist may recommend monitoring, especially if you take other medications or have liver concerns.

Laser treatment

Laser therapy targets the fungus with focused light energy and is a popular option for people who want an in-office procedure with no downtime — a typical session takes about 10 to 20 minutes. Most patients need multiple sessions spaced a few weeks apart for the best results, and because it's often considered cosmetic, insurance coverage for laser treatment is inconsistent, so it's worth checking with your provider beforehand.

Surgical nail removal

For nails that are severely infected, thickened, or painful, partial or complete surgical removal of the nail may be recommended, often combined with topical or oral antifungal treatment to clear the infection at the nail bed. This is a well-tolerated, in-office procedure performed under local anesthetic. New nail growth is a gradual process and can take roughly 9 to 12 months to fully grow back.

What about home remedies?

Home remedies such as vinegar soaks, tea tree oil, or over-the-counter creams are popular online, but they generally aren't strong enough to fully clear an established fungal infection. They may temporarily improve the nail's appearance without addressing the fungus underneath, which can allow the infection to persist or worsen. If you've tried home care without success, it's a good sign it's time to see a podiatrist.

Choosing the right treatment for you

The best option depends on how severe the infection is, how many nails are affected, your overall health, and your tolerance for the time commitment each treatment requires. A podiatrist can examine the nail — and in some cases send a sample for testing to confirm it's truly a fungal infection — before recommending a plan. Combining treatments, such as oral medication with a topical, is common for more stubborn infections.

Preventing toenail fungus from coming back

Because fungus thrives in warm, damp environments, prevention is largely about keeping your feet dry and your shoes rotated. Helpful habits include:

  • Keeping feet clean and thoroughly dried, especially between the toes
  • Wearing moisture-wicking socks and rotating shoes so they can fully dry between wears
  • Wearing sandals in public showers, locker rooms, and pool areas
  • Not sharing nail clippers or files with others
  • Monitoring treated nails for signs of recurrence, since fungal infections can return

Common Questions

Toenail fungus FAQs

Can toenail fungus go away without treatment?

No, toenail fungus does not typically resolve on its own. Without treatment, it can slowly worsen and spread to other nails or the surrounding skin, which is why it's best addressed once you notice changes in the nail's color, thickness, or texture.

How long does it take to treat toenail fungus?

It varies by treatment. Topical treatments often take several months since the nail has to grow out clear. Oral medications typically run a few weeks to a few months. Laser treatment involves multiple short sessions, and surgical removal is followed by 9 to 12 months of new nail growth.

Is laser treatment for toenail fungus covered by insurance?

Coverage is inconsistent because laser treatment is often classified as cosmetic. It's a good idea to check with your insurance provider and our office before scheduling to understand your out-of-pocket cost.

Do home remedies work for toenail fungus?

Home remedies like vinegar soaks or tea tree oil generally aren't strong enough to fully clear an established fungal infection. They may improve the nail's appearance temporarily without treating the underlying fungus, so persistent infections should be evaluated by a podiatrist.

Will my nail grow back normal after surgical removal?

In most cases, yes — once the infection is cleared, a new, healthy nail typically grows in over roughly 9 to 12 months. Continuing good foot hygiene during this time helps prevent the fungus from returning.

When should I see a podiatrist about toenail fungus?

See a podiatrist if a nail is discolored, thickened, brittle, or painful, or if you've tried over-the-counter or home remedies without improvement. Early treatment is generally easier and faster than treating an advanced infection. You can request an appointment at our Farmington or South Ogden office.
