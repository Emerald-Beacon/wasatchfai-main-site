Do Heel Injections Help With Heel Pain?

Heel pain has more than one cause, and steroid injections are just one tool in the toolbox. Here's how heel pain is diagnosed and when an injection actually makes sense.

By the physicians at Wasatch Foot & Ankle Institute · Updated July 13, 2026

Heel pain can come from several different sources — heel spurs, arthritis, an underlying injury, or tendonitis — and the right treatment depends on which one is behind your symptoms. Steroid injections are one option that can help when more conservative measures haven't been enough, but they're rarely the first step. Here's what causes heel pain, what treatments are typically tried first, and when an injection makes sense.

Heel pain is one of the most common reasons patients visit our Farmington and South Ogden offices. It can range from a mild ache after a long day on your feet to a sharp pain that makes every step uncomfortable. Understanding what's driving your particular pain is the first step toward treating it effectively.

What causes heel pain?

Several conditions can produce pain in or around the heel, and they don't always feel the same:

  • Heel spurs — a calcium deposit that builds up on the underside of the heel bone. Spurs vary in size, and while many people have one without any pain at all, larger or more irritated spurs can be a source of discomfort.
  • Arthritis of the foot — inflammation in the joints of the foot can cause heel pain directly, or indirectly by changing the way you walk to compensate for pain elsewhere.
  • Heel injuries — a stress fracture or other injury, often related to how the foot lands during running, jumping, or other physical activity.
  • Tendonitis — inflammation of the tendons around the heel, which can produce pain that worsens with activity.

Because these causes call for different treatment approaches, an accurate diagnosis from a podiatrist is the foundation of effective heel pain care. See our plantar fasciitis page if your pain is sharpest with your first steps in the morning, which points toward that specific condition.

What treatments are tried before an injection?

We generally start with the most conservative options and move to more targeted treatment if pain persists. Common first-line approaches include:

Icing and pain management

Icing the heel and using over-the-counter anti-inflammatory medication can reduce swelling and take the edge off acute pain, particularly in the early stages.

Physical therapy

Stretching exercises and gait modification can relieve unnecessary stress on the heel and address some of the underlying mechanical issues that contribute to pain.

Custom shoes or inserts

Orthopedic support — including custom orthotics — can reduce the stress placed on your heel during daily activities and help distribute weight more evenly across the foot.

When do heel injections help?

When icing, physical therapy, and supportive footwear haven't provided enough relief, a steroid injection is an option that can help calm persistent inflammation and reduce pain. Injections are typically considered a more targeted step after conservative measures have been tried, rather than a first-line treatment, and your podiatrist can help determine whether one is appropriate for your specific cause of heel pain.

Why prompt evaluation matters

Heel pain that's left untreated can worsen over time and may lead to changes in how you walk, which can create new problems elsewhere in the foot, ankle, knee, or hip. Prompt evaluation by a podiatrist helps identify the underlying cause early, when it's often easier to treat. If your heel pain is persistent, worsening, or interfering with your daily activities, it's worth having it evaluated rather than waiting it out.

Common Questions

Heel injection FAQs

What is a heel injection made of?

Heel injections for pain typically contain a corticosteroid, which reduces inflammation, sometimes combined with a local anesthetic. Your podiatrist will discuss the specific medication used based on your diagnosis.

Are heel injections the first treatment tried for heel pain?

No. Icing, anti-inflammatory medication, physical therapy, and supportive footwear or orthotics are usually tried first. Injections are typically considered when those conservative measures haven't provided enough relief.

What conditions can cause heel pain besides plantar fasciitis?

Heel pain can also come from heel spurs, arthritis of the foot, heel injuries such as stress fractures, and tendonitis in the tendons around the heel. Identifying the specific cause is important for choosing the right treatment.

How long does relief from a heel injection last?

Relief varies from person to person and depends on the underlying cause of the pain. Some patients get weeks to months of relief, while others may need additional treatment alongside the injection to address the root cause.

Is a heel spur always the cause of heel pain?

Not necessarily. Heel spurs vary in size, and many people have one without experiencing any pain at all. When a spur is associated with pain, treating the underlying inflammation is often what provides relief.

When should I see a podiatrist about heel pain?

See a podiatrist if your heel pain is persistent, worsening, or interfering with daily activities. Prompt evaluation helps identify the cause early, when it's often easier to treat. You can request an appointment at our Farmington or South Ogden office.
