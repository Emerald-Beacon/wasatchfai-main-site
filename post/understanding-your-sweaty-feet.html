Understanding Your Sweaty Feet

Excessive foot sweating is more than a minor annoyance — it can be a medical condition called hyperhidrosis, and it can lead to real complications if it's ignored.

By the physicians at Wasatch Foot & Ankle Institute · Updated July 13, 2026

Ongoing, excessive sweating of the feet is known as hyperhidrosis. Unlike the normal sweating triggered by heat, exercise, or nerves, hyperhidrosis causes the feet to sweat far more than the body actually needs to regulate its temperature — sometimes even when the feet are cool. It's more than a cosmetic or comfort issue: persistent moisture creates an environment where skin infections, odor, and irritation are more likely to develop.

Nearly everyone's feet sweat sometimes. But for people with hyperhidrosis, that sweating is frequent, heavy, and often not tied to an obvious trigger like heat or activity. If soggy socks and damp shoes are a daily reality for you, here's what may be causing it and what actually helps.

What is considered "sweaty feet"?

Sweaty feet become a medical concern when the sweating is persistent and excessive rather than occasional. People with hyperhidrosis often notice that their feet sweat noticeably more than the people around them, even in similar conditions — at rest, in a cool room, or during ordinary daily activities that wouldn't normally cause much perspiration.

What causes sweaty feet?

Hyperhidrosis happens when the body's sweat response is overactive, triggering perspiration without a clear warranted reason. A number of factors can contribute to or worsen the condition, including:

  • Stress and anxiety, which can trigger the nervous system's sweat response
  • A genetic or family predisposition to excessive sweating
  • Underlying infections, including some fungal skin infections
  • Diabetes and other conditions that affect the nerves or circulation
  • Hormonal changes, such as those related to menstruation

Because some of these causes — like diabetes — carry their own risks for foot health, persistent sweaty feet are worth mentioning to your podiatrist rather than dismissing as a minor annoyance.

What complications can sweaty feet cause?

Constant moisture changes the environment on and around your skin, which can lead to problems beyond simple discomfort:

  • Skin infections — damp skin is more susceptible to bacterial and fungal growth, including conditions like nail infections and athlete's foot
  • Excessive foot odor, caused by bacteria breaking down sweat on the skin and inside footwear
  • Discomfort and skin breakdown from socks and shoes that stay damp, including blisters and chafing
  • Difficulty with footwear, since shoes and insoles can be damaged or degrade faster when consistently wet

How can you manage sweaty feet?

For most people, a combination of daily habits can meaningfully reduce sweating and lower the risk of complications:

  • Moisture-wicking socks. Athletic or technical socks pull moisture away from the skin better than cotton, which tends to stay wet.
  • Breathable footwear. Choose shoes made from materials that allow airflow, and rotate pairs so each one has a full day to dry out between wears.
  • Rigorous foot hygiene. Wash your feet daily with an antibacterial soap, and dry thoroughly — especially between the toes — before putting on socks.
  • Change socks during the day if your feet sweat heavily, to keep skin as dry as possible.
  • Antiperspirant products made for feet can help reduce sweat production for some people.

These steps help most people manage mild to moderate sweating. If your symptoms are severe, don't improve with home care, or come with signs of infection — redness, odor that doesn't resolve, peeling skin, or pain — it's time to see a specialist.

When should you see a podiatrist?

Persistent, heavy sweating that interferes with daily life or keeps leading to skin problems is worth a professional evaluation. A podiatrist can help identify any underlying cause, check for existing skin or nail infections, and discuss additional treatment options beyond home care. If you're dealing with sweaty feet that just won't let up, you can request an appointment at either of our Northern Utah offices.

Common Questions

Sweaty feet FAQs

What is hyperhidrosis?

Hyperhidrosis is a condition of persistent, excessive sweating that goes beyond what the body needs to regulate temperature. When it affects the feet, it can happen even when they're cool or at rest, unlike normal sweating from heat or exercise.

Are sweaty feet a sign of a health problem?

Not always — some people simply sweat more than others. But sweaty feet can also be linked to stress, hormonal changes, infections, or conditions like diabetes, so persistent symptoms are worth discussing with a podiatrist.

Can sweaty feet cause infections?

Yes. Constant moisture creates conditions where bacteria and fungi are more likely to grow, which can lead to problems like athlete's foot, nail infections, and unpleasant odor.

What socks are best for sweaty feet?

Moisture-wicking athletic or technical socks are generally better than cotton, since they pull sweat away from the skin instead of holding onto it. Changing socks during the day can also help if sweating is heavy.

How do I stop my feet from smelling?

Foot odor is usually caused by bacteria breaking down sweat. Washing daily with antibacterial soap, drying thoroughly, wearing moisture-wicking socks, and rotating breathable shoes so they can fully dry between wears all help reduce odor.

When should I see a podiatrist about sweaty feet?

See a podiatrist if sweating is severe, doesn't improve with home care, or comes with signs of infection such as redness, persistent odor, peeling skin, or pain. You can request an appointment at our Farmington or South Ogden office.
