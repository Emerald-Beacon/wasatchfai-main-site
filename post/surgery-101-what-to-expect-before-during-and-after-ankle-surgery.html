Surgery 101: What to Expect Before, During, and After Ankle Surgery

Facing ankle surgery can feel overwhelming. Here is a clear, step-by-step look at how our team prepares you, what happens in the operating room, and what recovery really looks like.

By the physicians at Wasatch Foot & Ankle Institute · Updated July 13, 2026

Ankle surgery generally falls into two categories: open surgery, which uses a larger incision for direct visualization of the joint, and arthroscopic surgery, a minimally invasive approach that uses small incisions and specialized instruments. Most patients recover in six to eight weeks for the initial healing phase, with full rehabilitation sometimes taking several months. Knowing what to expect at each stage — before, during, and after — can make the entire process far less stressful.

Whether you are having surgery to repair a fracture, reconstruct a ligament, or address chronic ankle instability, the path from consultation to full recovery follows a predictable pattern. Below, our podiatric surgeons in Farmington and South Ogden walk through each phase.

Open surgery vs. arthroscopic surgery: what's the difference?

Open surgery involves a larger incision that allows the surgeon to directly see and access the ankle joint. It is typically recommended for more complex repairs, such as significant fractures or reconstructions that require extensive work on bone or soft tissue.

Arthroscopic surgery is a minimally invasive alternative. The surgeon works through a few small incisions using a tiny camera (arthroscope) and specialized instruments. Because the incisions are smaller, arthroscopic procedures commonly result in less scarring and a faster initial recovery than open surgery. Your surgeon will recommend the approach that best fits your specific injury.

How do you prepare for ankle surgery?

Before surgery, you will go through a pre-surgical evaluation to confirm you are ready for the procedure and to plan the surgical approach. This typically includes:

  • Medical evaluation to review your overall health and any conditions that could affect surgery or healing
  • Imaging such as X-rays, MRI, or CT scans to map out the extent of the injury
  • A team consultation with your orthopedic surgeon and, when needed, an anesthesiologist to plan the procedure
  • A discussion of anesthesia options — general anesthesia, which puts you fully to sleep, or regional anesthesia, a nerve block paired with light sedation

This is also the time to ask questions about recovery, work restrictions, and what kind of help you will need at home afterward.

What happens on the day of surgery?

Most ankle surgeries take between one and two hours, though complex reconstructions may take longer. You will check in ahead of time for final preparation, receive your chosen anesthesia, and the surgical team will complete the procedure using the open or arthroscopic approach determined during your consultation. Afterward, you will move to a recovery area where the team monitors you as the anesthesia wears off before you are discharged with initial care instructions.

What does recovery from ankle surgery look like?

Recovery happens in phases. The initial healing period generally lasts six to eight weeks, while full rehabilitation and return to normal activity can take several months depending on the procedure and how closely you follow your recovery plan. Key parts of recovery include:

  • Pain management using prescribed medication and, over time, over-the-counter options as needed
  • Wound care to keep the incision clean and reduce infection risk
  • Assistive devices such as crutches, a knee scooter, or a walking boot while weight-bearing is restricted
  • Physical therapy to rebuild strength, flexibility, and range of motion once your surgeon clears you
  • Good nutrition to support tissue healing

Your surgeon will give you a personalized timeline for when you can begin bearing weight, driving, and returning to work or sport — sticking to that plan is one of the biggest factors in a smooth recovery.

Why preparation matters

Patients who understand what to expect at each stage — and who follow their surgeon's pre- and post-operative instructions closely — tend to have smoother, more predictable recoveries. Common Questions

Ankle surgery FAQs

How long does ankle surgery take?

Most ankle surgeries take between one and two hours. More complex reconstructions involving multiple ligaments, fractures, or bone grafting may take longer. Your surgeon can give you a more specific estimate based on your procedure.

How long until I can put weight on my ankle after surgery?

Weight-bearing restrictions typically last two to six weeks, depending on the procedure performed and how your healing progresses. Your surgeon will tell you exactly when it is safe to begin putting weight on the ankle and how to progress from there.

What is the difference between open and arthroscopic ankle surgery?

Open surgery uses a larger incision for direct visualization of the joint and is typically used for more complex repairs. Arthroscopic surgery is minimally invasive, using small incisions and a camera, which commonly means less scarring and a faster initial recovery.

Can I drive after ankle surgery?

You should not drive while taking prescription pain medication or while wearing a restrictive cast or boot that limits your ability to safely operate a vehicle. Your surgeon will let you know when it is safe to resume driving.

How long is the full recovery from ankle surgery?

Initial healing generally takes six to eight weeks, but full rehabilitation — including regaining strength, flexibility, and a return to normal activity — can take several months depending on the procedure and your individual healing.

What type of anesthesia is used for ankle surgery?

Ankle surgery typically uses either general anesthesia, which puts you fully to sleep, or regional anesthesia, a nerve block combined with light sedation. Your surgical team will discuss which option is best for your procedure and health history.
