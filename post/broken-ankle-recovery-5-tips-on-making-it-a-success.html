Broken Ankle Recovery: 5 Tips on Making It a Success

A fractured ankle can sideline you for weeks — these five practical habits help you heal on schedule and avoid the complications that slow recovery down.

By the physicians at Wasatch Foot & Ankle Institute · Updated July 13, 2026

Most ankle fractures heal within 6 to 12 weeks, though the exact timeline depends on the severity of the break, your overall health, and your age. Following PRICE therapy, using the right recovery equipment, progressing weight-bearing activity carefully, watching closely for warning signs, and rebuilding strength afterward all help keep your recovery on track.

A broken ankle is one of the more disruptive injuries we treat — it changes how you get around, get dressed, and get through your day. The good news is that most patients who break an ankle go on to heal well and return to their normal activities. How smoothly that recovery goes often comes down to a handful of habits in the weeks after your injury or surgery. Here are five tips our podiatrists share most often with patients recovering from ankle fractures.

1. Follow PRICE therapy faithfully

In the early stages of recovery, PRICE — Protection, Rest, Ice, Compression, and Elevation — is the foundation of healing. Protect the ankle in the brace, boot, or cast your provider recommends, and avoid the temptation to test it early. Ice packs (velcro straps make them easy to keep in place) and elevation above heart level several times a day help control swelling, while over-the-counter pain relievers like ibuprofen can help manage discomfort as directed by your provider.

2. Set yourself up with the right equipment

Small purchases can make a big difference during the non-weight-bearing stage of recovery. A shower bench or chair makes bathing safer when you cannot stand on the injured leg, and a structured ankle brace can protect the joint while you sleep. Loose-fitting pants with wide bottoms make dressing easier over a boot or cast, and a temporary handicap parking placard can reduce unnecessary strain during errands. Crutches or a knee scooter, fitted properly, also make a noticeable difference in how manageable daily life feels.

3. Progress weight-bearing activities slowly and safely

Do not put weight on the injured ankle until your surgeon or physical therapist gives you the green light. Once you are cleared, ease in — start with just 5 to 10 minutes of light weight-bearing at a time and increase gradually as your provider approves. Rushing this stage is one of the most common ways patients set their own recovery back, so resist the urge to compare your progress to anyone else's timeline.

4. Monitor symptoms closely

Keep an eye on how your ankle looks and feels throughout recovery, and report changes to your provider right away. Contact your podiatrist promptly if you notice fever, numbness or tingling, increasing swelling or pain, or any change in skin color around the injury. Catching complications early — whether it is a healing problem, infection, or circulation issue — makes them far easier to manage than waiting until symptoms worsen.

5. Take steps to prevent future injuries

Once you are cleared to bear weight again, cross-training on low-impact activities can help you rebuild strength without overloading the healing bone. A balanced diet rich in calcium and vitamin D supports bone healing, though it is best to avoid excessive vitamin A intake, which some research links to reduced bone density. Many patients also benefit from a period of physical therapy focused on rebuilding ankle strength and balance, since a previous fracture can raise the risk of future sprains or lateral ankle instability. Supportive footwear or custom orthotics can also help protect the joint as you return to full activity.

For more guidance through every stage of healing, visit our recovery resources, or reach out if you have questions specific to your injury — every fracture is a little different, and your recovery plan should be too.

Common Questions

Broken ankle recovery FAQs

How long does it take to recover from a broken ankle?

Most ankle fractures heal within 6 to 12 weeks, though the timeline depends on the severity of the break, whether surgery was needed, your overall health, and your age. Your provider will confirm healing progress with follow-up imaging before clearing you for full activity.

What is PRICE therapy for a broken ankle?

PRICE stands for Protection, Rest, Ice, Compression, and Elevation. It is the standard early-recovery approach for controlling swelling and pain after an ankle fracture, and it is typically used alongside a brace, boot, or cast recommended by your provider.

When can I start putting weight on a broken ankle?

Only when your surgeon or physical therapist gives you the green light. Weight-bearing typically begins with short sessions of just 5 to 10 minutes at minimal weight, then increases gradually as your provider approves based on how well the bone is healing.

What warning signs should I watch for during ankle fracture recovery?

Contact your podiatrist promptly if you notice fever, numbness or tingling, increasing swelling or pain, or any change in skin color around the injury. These can be early signs of a healing problem, infection, or circulation issue that is easier to manage the sooner it is caught.

How can I prevent another ankle injury after I've healed?

Cross-training on low-impact activities, physical therapy to rebuild strength and balance, and a diet rich in calcium and vitamin D all support long-term ankle health. Supportive footwear or custom orthotics can also help protect a previously fractured ankle from future sprains.

Do I need custom orthotics after a broken ankle?

Not everyone does, but many patients benefit from added support as they return to full activity, especially if the fracture changed their gait or balance. A podiatrist can evaluate whether custom orthotics would help protect your ankle during this stage.
