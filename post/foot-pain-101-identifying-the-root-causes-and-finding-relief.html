Foot Pain 101: Identifying the Root Causes and Finding Relief

Where does it hurt? The location of your foot pain is often the first clue to what's causing it — and the fastest path to real relief.

By the physicians at Wasatch Foot & Ankle Institute · Updated July 13, 2026

Foot pain isn't one condition — it's a symptom with dozens of possible causes, and where it hurts is often the biggest clue to what's going on. Heel pain, arch pain, ball-of-foot pain, and toe pain each point toward a different set of likely culprits. Understanding the pattern of your pain is the first step toward real, lasting relief instead of just masking the symptoms.

Our podiatrists evaluate foot pain every day, and while a proper diagnosis always requires an exam, this guide walks through the most common causes by location so you have a starting point for understanding what you're dealing with.

Heel pain

Heel pain is one of the most common complaints we see. Plantar fasciitis — inflammation of the band of tissue along the bottom of the foot — is the leading cause, producing sharp pain that's worst with your first steps in the morning. Heel spurs, Achilles tendon irritation at the back of the heel, and stress fractures can also cause heel pain, each with slightly different patterns and triggers.

Arch pain

Pain along the arch is often related to the same structures involved in plantar fasciitis, but it can also point to posterior tibial tendon dysfunction — a condition where the tendon that supports the arch becomes irritated or weakened, sometimes leading to a flattening of the arch over time. Flat feet or unsupportive footwear can contribute to ongoing arch strain.

Ball-of-foot pain

Pain under the ball of the foot, sometimes called metatarsalgia, often results from how weight is distributed across the front of the foot. High-impact activity, tight or narrow shoes, thinning of the natural fat pad with age, or structural issues like a bunion or hammertoe can all concentrate pressure in this area and lead to persistent aching or a feeling of "walking on a pebble."

Toe pain

Toe pain has a wide range of causes. A bunion — an enlarged, often painful joint at the base of the big toe — is common, especially with narrow footwear. Sudden, severe pain and swelling in the big toe joint can signal gout, caused by uric acid crystal buildup. Ingrown toenails, hammertoes, and hallux rigidus (stiffness and pain in the big toe joint) are other frequent sources of toe pain.

Widespread or joint-related pain

If pain, stiffness, and swelling affect multiple joints in the foot, especially in the morning or after rest, arthritis may be the underlying cause. Rheumatoid arthritis and osteoarthritis can both affect the foot and ankle, and each is managed differently — so an accurate diagnosis matters.

Finding real relief

Because so many conditions can cause foot pain, a proper diagnosis is the foundation of effective treatment. In the meantime, rest, ice, supportive footwear, and over-the-counter anti-inflammatory medication can help manage mild symptoms. But relief that lasts usually requires treating the actual cause — whether that's targeted stretching for plantar fasciitis, custom orthotics for structural support, or medical management for arthritis or gout.

When to see a podiatrist

See a podiatrist if pain lasts more than a week or two despite rest, limits your daily activities, or comes with swelling, redness, numbness, or a visible deformity. People with diabetes or circulation problems should have any new foot pain checked promptly, since minor issues can become serious more quickly. Common Questions

Foot pain FAQs

What does it mean if my heel hurts most in the morning?

Sharp heel pain with your first steps in the morning is a classic sign of plantar fasciitis, caused by inflammation of the band of tissue along the bottom of the foot. The pain often eases as you move, then returns after long periods of standing or rest.

Why does the ball of my foot hurt when I walk?

Pain concentrated under the ball of the foot, sometimes called metatarsalgia, is often related to how weight is distributed across the front of the foot. It can be caused by ill-fitting shoes, high-impact activity, thinning of the natural fat pad, or structural issues like a bunion or hammertoe.

Could my foot pain be arthritis?

Arthritis is a common cause of foot pain, especially if you notice stiffness, swelling, or aching in the joints that is worse in the morning or after periods of rest. It can affect any joint in the foot or ankle, and a podiatrist can help identify the type and best treatment approach.

Is it normal for foot pain to come and go?

Foot pain that flares with certain activities and eases with rest is common with overuse injuries like tendonitis or plantar fasciitis. However, pain that keeps returning is a sign the underlying cause hasn't been resolved, and it's worth having it evaluated rather than only treating the symptoms as they come up.

When is foot pain serious enough to see a podiatrist?

See a podiatrist if pain lasts more than a week or two, limits your ability to walk or do daily activities, or is accompanied by swelling, redness, numbness, or a visible deformity. People with diabetes or poor circulation should have any new foot pain evaluated promptly.
