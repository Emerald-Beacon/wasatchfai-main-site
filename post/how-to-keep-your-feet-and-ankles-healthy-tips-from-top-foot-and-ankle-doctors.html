How to Keep Your Feet and Ankles Healthy: Tips from Top Foot and Ankle Doctors

Your feet carry you through every day of your life. Here are five doctor-backed habits that keep them — and your ankles — strong for the long run.

By the physicians at Wasatch Foot & Ankle Institute · Updated July 13, 2026

Healthy feet and ankles do not happen by accident — they are the result of a handful of everyday habits: the right footwear, good hygiene, regular movement, solid nutrition, and prompt attention when something feels off. Because your feet absorb impact with every single step, small neglect adds up over years into bigger problems.

Our podiatrists in Farmington and South Ogden see the full range, from minor irritations that could have been prevented to serious conditions that started as something small. Below are five practical, doctor-backed habits that go a long way toward keeping your feet and ankles healthy for life.

1. Wear proper, well-fitting footwear

Ill-fitting shoes are one of the most common causes of foot problems we see, contributing to blisters, calluses, corns, and even structural issues over time. Look for shoes with a supportive arch, a stable heel, and enough room in the toe box that your toes are not compressed. Replace athletic shoes regularly, since worn-out cushioning and support lose their protective effect long before the shoe looks obviously damaged. If you have flat feet, high arches, or a diagnosed alignment issue, custom orthotics can add support that off-the-shelf shoes cannot.

2. Practice good foot hygiene

Daily hygiene habits prevent a surprising number of foot problems. Wash your feet regularly, dry them thoroughly — especially between the toes — and moisturize to prevent cracking, particularly around the heels. Trim toenails straight across rather than rounding the corners, which helps prevent ingrown nails. Good hygiene also reduces your risk of fungal infections, which thrive in warm, damp environments like the inside of a shoe.

3. Stay physically active

Regular movement keeps the muscles, tendons, and ligaments in your feet and ankles strong and flexible. Simple exercises like toe raises, toe curls, and ankle circles can improve strength and range of motion in just a few minutes a day. If you have a sedentary routine, low-impact activities such as walking, swimming, or cycling are a good way to build foot and ankle strength without excessive strain.

4. Support your bones with good nutrition

What you eat plays a role in foot health, too. A diet rich in calcium and vitamin D supports bone strength, which matters for a part of the body that bears your full weight all day. Reducing excess sugar and sodium can also help limit inflammation, which is a factor in conditions such as gout and rheumatoid arthritis.

5. Get professional attention early

Persistent foot or ankle pain is never something to just push through. Early intervention can prevent a minor issue from becoming a more severe, harder-to-treat problem — whether that is a stress fracture, tendon injury, or a structural issue that is changing how you walk. This is especially important for people with diabetes, who should have any new foot symptom evaluated promptly given the higher risk of complications. See our diabetic foot care guidance for more on that.

None of these habits require a major lifestyle overhaul — they are small, sustainable choices that protect the two feet and ankles you will rely on for the rest of your life. If you are dealing with foot or ankle pain that is not improving, or you simply want a baseline check-up, you can request an appointment at either of our Northern Utah offices.

Common Questions

Foot and ankle health FAQs

What is the single most important habit for foot health?

There is no single most important habit — footwear, hygiene, activity, nutrition, and prompt medical attention all work together. Wearing properly fitting, supportive shoes is one of the easiest places to start since it affects your feet with every step you take.

How often should I replace my athletic shoes?

Most athletic shoes lose meaningful cushioning and support well before they look visibly worn out. If you are active regularly, plan to replace running or walking shoes every few hundred miles or when you notice reduced support, even if the exterior still looks fine.

What exercises help keep feet and ankles strong?

Simple movements like toe raises, toe curls, and ankle circles help build strength and flexibility in just a few minutes a day. Low-impact activities such as walking, swimming, or cycling are also effective, especially if your daily routine is largely sedentary.

Can diet really affect foot health?

Yes. Calcium and vitamin D support the bone strength your feet need to bear your full body weight every day, while reducing excess sugar and sodium can help limit inflammation associated with conditions like gout and rheumatoid arthritis.

Why is early treatment for foot pain so important?

Foot and ankle problems often get harder to treat the longer they are ignored, and untreated pain can change how you walk, creating new issues elsewhere in the body. Early evaluation gives you more treatment options and generally leads to a faster, easier recovery.

When should I see a podiatrist instead of waiting it out?

See a podiatrist if pain persists beyond a week or two of home care, limits your daily activities, or keeps recurring. People with diabetes or circulation issues should have any new foot symptom checked promptly. You can request an appointment at our Farmington or South Ogden office.
