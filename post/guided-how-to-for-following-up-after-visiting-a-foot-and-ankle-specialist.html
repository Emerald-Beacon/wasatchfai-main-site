Your Follow-Up Guide After Visiting a Foot and Ankle Specialist

Your appointment doesn't end your recovery — it starts it. Here's how to track symptoms, time your next visit, and communicate with your care team along the way.

By the physicians at Wasatch Foot & Ankle Institute · Updated July 13, 2026

Following up after a visit to a foot and ankle specialist is just as important as the initial appointment. Whether you're recovering from an injury, managing a chronic condition, or working through plantar fasciitis, ankle instability, or heel pain, what you do between visits shapes how well — and how quickly — you heal. Here's a practical guide to staying on track after you leave our office.

It's easy to walk out of an appointment feeling relieved the visit is over, then lose momentum on the plan your podiatrist gave you. A little organization goes a long way: knowing what to track, when to reach back out, and how to communicate clearly can make the difference between a smooth recovery and a stalled one.

Start by clarifying your diagnosis and treatment plan

Before you leave the office, make sure you understand your specific diagnosis and the reasoning behind your treatment plan. If your provider uses a medical term you don't recognize, write it down so you can ask about it later — either at your next visit or through your patient portal. A clear understanding of what's being treated and why makes it much easier to follow through at home.

Why follow-up communication matters

Recovery doesn't stop when the appointment ends. Symptoms can change day to day, and new developments — a flare-up, an unexpected reaction to a brace or orthotic, or pain that isn't improving as expected — are exactly the kind of information your provider needs to adjust your care. Staying in communication lets your treatment plan evolve with your recovery instead of staying frozen at whatever was decided during a single visit.

How soon should you schedule your follow-up?

Timing depends on what you're being treated for. As a general guide:

  • Post-operative care typically calls for a follow-up within 1–2 weeks of your procedure
  • Chronic conditions such as arthritis are often reassessed on a roughly monthly basis
  • Worsening symptoms at any point warrant reaching out sooner rather than waiting for your scheduled visit

If you're ever unsure whether something you're feeling is "normal" for your recovery stage, it's always reasonable to ask.

Choosing the right way to reach your provider

Not every question needs a phone call, and not every concern can wait for email. A few guidelines:

  • Patient portal or email — best for non-urgent questions, prescription refill requests, and general updates
  • Phone call — best for urgent concerns, such as sudden increases in pain, swelling, or signs of infection
  • Secure practice portal — convenient for scheduling appointments and managing prescriptions in one place

Organize your questions and concerns before you reach out

Whether you're messaging your provider or preparing for your next appointment, it helps to have your thoughts organized in advance. Before you contact the office, jot down:

  • Any changes in your pain — better, worse, or different in character or location
  • How your exercise or activity tolerance has changed since your last visit
  • Questions about insurance coverage for treatments, orthotics, or procedures

Preparing for your next appointment

Come to each follow-up equipped with a brief summary of how things have gone: what's improved, what hasn't, and any new symptoms. If you've been given exercises, orthotics, or medication, note how consistently you've been able to follow the plan. Bring any relevant documentation, such as notes from physical therapy or a symptom log (more on that below) — it helps your provider make informed decisions quickly.

Tracking your symptoms and progress

A simple daily log can make a real difference in your care. Consider tracking:

  • Pain level on a 1–10 scale, noted at consistent times of day
  • What activities seem to trigger or worsen symptoms
  • Improvements in mobility, swelling, or overall function

Patterns that are hard to remember from a single follow-up conversation often become obvious once they're written down — and that record is genuinely useful to your provider.

Leaning on support resources during recovery

Recovering from a foot or ankle injury or managing a chronic condition can be frustrating, especially if progress feels slow. Support groups and community resources — whether centered on your specific condition or on injury recovery generally — can provide both emotional support and practical tips from people who've been through something similar.

Navigating insurance and financial questions

Before and between appointments, it's worth reviewing your insurance coverage details, any referral requirements for specialists or procedures, and keeping your paperwork organized. Our office staff can help answer coverage questions, and having your documentation in order ahead of time helps avoid delays in care.

Common Questions

Follow-up care FAQs

How soon should I schedule a follow-up after foot or ankle surgery?

Post-operative follow-ups are typically scheduled within 1–2 weeks of your procedure, though your surgeon may recommend a different timeline depending on the procedure. Contact the office sooner if you notice increasing pain, swelling, or signs of infection.

How often should I be seen for a chronic condition like arthritis?

Chronic conditions are often reassessed on a roughly monthly basis, though some patients are seen every 4–6 weeks depending on how their symptoms are progressing. Your provider will set a schedule that fits your specific situation.

What signs suggest my recovery is on track?

Decreasing pain levels, improved mobility, and a growing tolerance for daily activities or exercise are all good signs. A symptom log can help you and your provider spot these trends more clearly than relying on memory alone.

What should I do if my orthotics or brace feel uncomfortable?

Report discomfort from orthotics, braces, or other devices right away rather than waiting for your next scheduled visit. Ongoing discomfort can affect how well the device works and how consistently you're able to wear it.

Should I message my provider or schedule an appointment?

Non-urgent questions and updates are well suited to a patient portal message or email. If your symptoms are worsening or you're experiencing something new and concerning, it's better to call the office or schedule an appointment rather than relying on messaging alone.

What should I bring to my follow-up appointment?

Bring a brief summary of your symptoms since your last visit, notes on how well you've been able to follow your treatment plan, and any relevant documentation such as physical therapy notes or a symptom log. This helps your provider make informed decisions quickly.
