How Custom Orthotics Can Benefit You

Custom orthotics don't just cushion your step — they correct how your foot strikes the ground, easing strain that can travel all the way up to your knees, hips, and back.

By the physicians at Wasatch Foot & Ankle Institute · Updated July 13, 2026

Custom orthotics are medical-grade shoe inserts, molded from an imprint of your own feet, that precisely control how your foot strikes the ground with every step. Unlike over-the-counter insoles, which mainly add cushioning, custom orthotics correct alignment issues at the source — which is why they can ease pain not just in the foot, but in the knees, hips, and lower back. If you've been told your foot pain is "just how your feet are," a well-made pair of custom orthotics is often the missing piece.

Your feet are the foundation for everything above them. Every step sends force up through your ankles, knees, hips, and spine, and if your foot doesn't strike the ground evenly, that misalignment compounds with every stride you take in a day. Below, our podiatrists explain how custom orthotics work, who benefits most, and what to expect if you get a pair.

What makes custom orthotics different from store-bought inserts?

Over-the-counter insoles are built to a generic shape and mostly add padding. Custom orthotics are built from a precise imprint or scan of your own foot, then shaped to support your specific arch height, heel position, and gait pattern. That means they don't just feel more comfortable — they change the mechanics of how you walk, treating the root cause of many types of foot pain rather than simply masking the symptoms.

How do custom orthotics support your alignment?

Because your feet are your body's foundation, small imbalances in how they strike the ground don't stay isolated — they transfer up the kinetic chain. Custom orthotics maintain the natural curve of your arch during weight-bearing activity, which helps the foot absorb shock the way it's supposed to and keeps that force from concentrating in the wrong places. Over time, that can mean less compounding strain on your ankles, knees, hips, and lower back.

What conditions can custom orthotics help with?

Because they address the underlying mechanics rather than just covering up discomfort, custom orthotics are a common part of treatment plans for several conditions we see regularly, including:

  • Plantar fasciitis and general heel pain
  • Achilles tendonitis
  • Shin splints
  • Flat feet and fallen arches
  • Knee and lower-leg pain linked to how your foot strikes the ground

Custom orthotics aren't reserved only for people with a diagnosed condition. Many patients wear them simply to correct an inefficient gait, reduce daily fatigue, or support their feet through demanding jobs or training schedules.

Can custom orthotics improve athletic performance?

Yes — by limiting excess motion in the foot and improving how efficiently you move, custom orthotics can help runners and athletes maintain better stability, particularly during quick directional changes. Many patients also notice less overall fatigue during long days on their feet, since a well-aligned foot simply works less hard to compensate.

How long do custom orthotics last, and what should you expect?

Most people need an adjustment period of one to two weeks as their feet and gait adapt to the new support — mild soreness during that window is normal and typically resolves on its own. With proper care, quality custom orthotics generally hold their shape and support for three to five years, well outlasting basic store-bought inserts, which tend to compress and lose their supportive structure much sooner.

How do you get a pair of custom orthotics?

Getting fitted starts with an evaluation of your foot structure and gait, often including a scan or mold of your feet, so the orthotics can be built to your exact alignment needs. Our physicians will also check whether an underlying condition — like flat feet, plantar fasciitis, or Achilles tendon irritation — is contributing to your symptoms, so your treatment plan addresses the full picture, not just the insert. You can request an appointment at either of our Northern Utah offices to get started.

Common Questions

Custom orthotics FAQs

Are custom orthotics worth it over drugstore inserts?

For many people, yes. Drugstore inserts mostly add cushioning to a generic shape, while custom orthotics are built to your specific foot structure and gait to correct alignment issues at the source. If your pain is related to how your foot strikes the ground rather than just needing more padding, a custom pair typically provides more meaningful, longer-lasting relief.

How long does it take to adjust to custom orthotics?

Most patients need about one to two weeks to fully adjust as their feet and walking pattern adapt to the new support. Mild soreness during that break-in period is normal. Wearing them consistently, rather than only occasionally, usually shortens the adjustment window.

Do I need a diagnosed condition to get custom orthotics?

No. While custom orthotics are commonly used to treat conditions like plantar fasciitis, Achilles tendonitis, and flat feet, many patients wear them simply to correct an inefficient gait, reduce fatigue, or support their feet through demanding activity — no diagnosis required.

How long do custom orthotics last?

With proper care, quality custom orthotics typically maintain their shape and support for three to five years — considerably longer than basic over-the-counter insoles, which tend to compress and break down much sooner.

Can custom orthotics help with knee or hip pain?

They can, when that pain is related to how your foot strikes the ground. Because forces travel up through the ankles, knees, hips, and spine with every step, correcting foot alignment with custom orthotics can reduce the compounding strain that contributes to pain further up the chain.

How do I get fitted for custom orthotics?

Fitting starts with an evaluation of your foot structure and gait, often including a scan or mold of your feet, so the orthotics can be built to your exact needs. You can request an appointment at our Farmington or South Ogden office to get started.
