The Wasatch Foot & Ankle Blog

Straightforward, medically reviewed articles on heel pain, bunions, ankle injuries, diabetic foot care, and everything in between — written by the podiatrists who treat these conditions every day in Farmington and South Ogden.

If you're dealing with heel pain, a stubborn bunion, a sprained ankle, or a diabetic foot concern, you probably have questions before you ever set foot in an office. That's what this library is for. Our board-certified, fellowship-trained podiatrists have put together more than a hundred articles covering the conditions we see most often in Northern Utah — what causes them, how we diagnose them, and what treatment actually looks like, from simple home care to advanced in-office procedures.

Every article here is written for patients, not clinicians — clear language, practical next steps, and honest guidance on when a symptom is worth a phone call versus a same-day visit. Browse by category below, or if something feels urgent, skip the reading and request an appointment — same-day visits are available for foot and ankle emergencies.

Common Questions

Blog FAQs

Who writes the articles on this blog?

Every article is medically reviewed by the board-certified, fellowship-trained podiatrists at Wasatch Foot & Ankle Institute. Our goal is to give patients in Farmington, South Ogden, and the surrounding communities clear, accurate information about the foot and ankle conditions we treat every day.

Can I use this blog to self-diagnose my foot or ankle pain?

These articles are meant for general education, not as a substitute for an in-person evaluation. Many foot and ankle conditions share overlapping symptoms, so the safest way to get an accurate diagnosis is a hands-on exam with one of our podiatrists. If you're unsure where to start, our services page lists the conditions we treat.

How often is the blog updated?

We regularly add and refresh articles to reflect current, standard-of-care treatment approaches and the questions our patients ask most. Check back periodically, or reach out directly if you can't find the topic you're looking for.

When should I stop reading and just call the office?

Call right away for severe pain, visible deformity, an open wound, signs of infection, numbness, or any injury following a fall or trauma — especially if you have diabetes or poor circulation. For less urgent concerns, such as heel pain or a stubborn ingrown toenail, it's still best to be seen if symptoms last more than a week or two. You can request an appointment or call the office nearest you.

Do you treat both adults and children?

Yes. We see patients of all ages, from children with early foot alignment concerns to active adults with sports injuries and older adults managing arthritis or diabetic foot care. Our blog includes articles addressing all of these age groups.
