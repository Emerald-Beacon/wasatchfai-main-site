Dual Syndesmosis TightRope

A modern, flexible fixation technique that stabilizes the ankle joint after a high ankle sprain or unstable fracture, allowing more natural movement during healing than traditional screws.

What is dual syndesmosis TightRope fixation?

The syndesmosis is the strong ligament complex that binds the two lower leg bones — the tibia and fibula — together just above the ankle joint. When this joint is torn or separated, most often from a high ankle sprain or an ankle fracture, the bones can shift out of their normal position and the ankle becomes unstable. Dual syndesmosis TightRope fixation is a surgical technique that uses two flexible, suture-based implants to hold the tibia and fibula in the correct position while the ligaments heal.

Unlike a traditional rigid screw, which locks the bones in a fixed position and is sometimes removed later, the TightRope system allows a small amount of natural motion between the bones as you walk, similar to how the joint moves normally. Using two TightRope devices, rather than one, provides added rotational stability for syndesmosis injuries that need extra support. This procedure is often considered alongside treatment for related injuries such as ankle fractures and lateral ankle instability.

What injuries lead to needing this procedure?

Dual syndesmosis TightRope fixation is used when the syndesmosis ligaments have been torn or the joint has become unstable, most commonly from:

  • A high ankle sprain that involves the ligaments above the ankle joint rather than the more common outer-ankle sprain
  • An ankle fracture that also disrupts the syndesmosis, such as certain rotational or high-energy injuries
  • Sports injuries involving a forceful twisting or outward-rotation mechanism
  • Chronic syndesmotic instability that has not resolved with nonsurgical treatment

Because these injuries can be subtle on initial exam, they are sometimes missed or under-treated as a standard ankle sprain, which is why a thorough evaluation matters when a "sprain" is unusually slow to improve.

How do we diagnose a syndesmosis injury?

Diagnosis starts with a physical exam that includes specific stress tests designed to reveal instability between the tibia and fibula, along with a review of how the injury occurred. Because syndesmosis injuries frequently occur alongside ankle fractures or ligament tears, we assess the whole ankle rather than just the area of pain.

Imaging plays a key role in confirming the diagnosis and planning treatment. Weight-bearing X-rays help reveal whether the joint has widened or shifted, and in some cases a CT scan or MRI is used to get a more detailed look at the ligaments and any associated fracture.

How is dual syndesmosis TightRope surgery performed?

The procedure is performed under anesthesia and typically involves small incisions rather than a large open approach. Your surgeon repositions the tibia and fibula into their normal alignment, then places two TightRope devices across the joint to hold the bones securely while still allowing the small, natural motion the joint needs. If there is an associated fracture, it is stabilized as part of the same surgery. Because the implants are designed to stay in place long-term, a separate removal surgery usually is not needed unless a specific problem develops.

What is recovery from dual syndesmosis TightRope surgery like?

Recovery follows a staged process. In the initial weeks after surgery, you will typically be non-weightbearing or limited to protected weightbearing in a boot or cast while the soft tissues begin to heal. As healing progresses, your surgeon will advance you toward full weightbearing, followed by a course of physical therapy to restore strength, range of motion, and balance.

Because every injury and patient is different, your surgeon will give you a personalized timeline for returning to walking, driving, and athletic activity. Following your specific weightbearing and activity restrictions closely gives the ligaments the best chance to heal in the correct position.

What are the benefits of the TightRope technique over traditional screw fixation?

Traditional syndesmosis screws hold the joint rigidly fixed, which can occasionally break or require a second surgery for removal before full weightbearing activity resumes. The TightRope system's flexible design is intended to more closely mimic the ankle's natural, slight motion between the tibia and fibula, and in most cases does not require a planned removal procedure. Your surgeon will discuss which fixation method — or combination of methods — is right for your specific injury.

When should you see a foot and ankle surgeon about a syndesmosis injury?

See a specialist if you have significant ankle pain and swelling above the ankle joint after a twisting injury, if a "sprained" ankle is not improving as expected, or if you have been told your ankle fracture may involve the syndesmosis. Early, accurate diagnosis of a syndesmosis injury makes a real difference in how well the ankle heals. When you are ready, request an appointment at either of our Northern Utah offices.

Common Questions

Dual syndesmosis TightRope FAQs

What is the syndesmosis, and why does it need surgery?

The syndesmosis is the strong ligament complex that holds the tibia and fibula together just above the ankle joint. When it is torn badly enough that the joint becomes unstable, surgery is often needed to restore the correct alignment and stability so the ankle can heal properly and bear weight safely.

How is a TightRope different from a traditional syndesmosis screw?

A traditional screw holds the tibia and fibula rigidly fixed and is sometimes removed in a second surgery before full activity resumes. The TightRope system is a flexible, suture-based implant that allows the small natural motion the joint needs and usually does not require planned removal.

Why are two TightRope devices used instead of one?

Using two TightRope implants provides added rotational stability for syndesmosis injuries that need extra support, helping keep the tibia and fibula properly aligned while the ligaments heal.

How long is recovery after dual syndesmosis TightRope surgery?

Recovery is staged, starting with a period of non-weightbearing or protected weightbearing in a boot or cast, followed by a gradual return to full weightbearing and a course of physical therapy. Your surgeon will give you a personalized timeline based on your specific injury.

Will the TightRope implants need to be removed later?

Usually not. The TightRope system is designed to stay in place long-term, so a separate removal surgery generally is not needed unless a specific problem develops.

How do I know if my ankle sprain is actually a syndesmosis injury?

A syndesmosis, or "high ankle," sprain causes pain higher up the ankle than a typical outer-ankle sprain and often heals more slowly than expected. If your sprain isn't improving on the usual timeline, it's worth having a foot and ankle specialist evaluate it.

When should I see a specialist about a possible syndesmosis injury?

See a specialist if you have significant swelling and pain above the ankle joint after a twisting injury, or if a sprained ankle isn't improving as expected. You can request an appointment at our Farmington or South Ogden office.
